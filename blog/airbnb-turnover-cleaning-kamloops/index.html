Airbnb Turnover Cleaning Checklist for Kamloops Hosts

A turnover is not a deep clean and it is not a move-out clean. It is a reset between guests, done against the clock, judged by whoever walks in next. Here is what it covers, what it costs, and where hosts most often get caught out.

What a Turnover Clean Actually Is

A turnover clean takes a unit from “someone just checked out” to “someone can check in”. That is a narrower job than it sounds, and the narrowness is the point: the property is already furnished, already maintained, and was clean a few days ago. Nothing has had time to accumulate.

What it is not is a recovery job. A turnover assumes reasonable condition. If a guest has left a unit in a genuinely bad state, that is not a turnover any more and it should not be quoted as one — more on that below.

It is also judged differently from a home clean. Nobody inspects their own kitchen the way a paying guest inspects an unfamiliar one. A turnover is as much about presentation as cleanliness: straight cushions, a made bed, no hair in the shower, nothing of the last guest left behind.

The Turnover Checklist, Room by Room

What gets covered on a standard turnover of a maintained unit.

Kitchen reset

  • Counters, sink and taps cleared and wiped down
  • Hob surface and splashback — cooking residue from the last stay
  • Outside of the fridge, microwave, kettle, toaster and any other appliance on show
  • Fridge checked and emptied of anything the guest left; interior wiped if needed
  • Dishes, cutlery and glassware checked and put away in the right places
  • Cupboard fronts and handles wiped
  • Bin emptied, liner replaced, bin itself wiped if it needs it
  • Floor swept and mopped

Bathroom reset

  • Toilet cleaned inside and out, including the base and behind
  • Shower or tub — surfaces, screen or curtain, drain checked for hair
  • Sink, vanity, taps and mirror
  • Floor cleaned, including the edges and behind the door
  • Towels replaced, toilet paper restocked, any amenities you supply replenished
  • Bin emptied

Bedrooms

  • Beds stripped and remade with fresh linen
  • Pillows checked, straightened and presented properly
  • Surfaces, nightstands and lamps dusted
  • Under the bed and behind the door checked for anything left behind
  • Wardrobes and drawers opened and checked, hangers reset
  • Floors vacuumed, including along the skirting

Living areas

  • Cushions straightened, throws folded, furniture returned to position
  • Surfaces, shelves, TV stand and remote controls wiped
  • Visible crumbs and debris cleared from sofas and chairs
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped

Throughout

  • High-touch points — door handles, light switches, railings
  • All bins emptied and liners replaced
  • Entry area and any glass on doors within reach
  • A final look round at guest eye level, which catches what a checklist does not

The visual check

The last step is the one that matters most and gets skipped most. Stand in each doorway and look at the room the way an arriving guest will: first impression, then detail. Hair on a bathroom floor, a smeared mirror, a crooked lampshade or a cushion out of place are the things that end up in a review, and none of them take more than a few seconds to fix if you are actually looking.

Linen and Laundry

The part of a turnover most likely to break a tight schedule.

Linen is the single biggest variable in turnover timing. Cleaning a unit takes a fairly predictable amount of time; washing and drying bedding does not, and it cannot be hurried.

Hosts generally run one of two systems. Either you keep two or three sets of linen per bed and the used set goes away to be washed while a fresh set goes on immediately, or you wash on site between stays and accept that the turnover now depends on a dryer. The first system is what makes same-day turnovers realistic; the second is what makes them fragile.

If you want laundry handled as part of the visit, it is priced as an add-on rather than built into the base rate, because plenty of units do not need it:

  • Laundry, first load — $30
  • Each additional load — $15
  • Linen change — $10 per bed

If you are running back-to-back bookings, the honest advice is to invest in spare linen sets before you invest in anything else. It removes the dryer from the critical path entirely.

Turnover Cleaning vs Move-Out Cleaning

These get confused constantly, and booking the wrong one costs either money or a bad review. They are different services at different rates.

Airbnb / rental turnover

Between guests, unit maintained

A furnished unit in reasonable condition, cleaned between stays. Presentation-focused, linen and amenities reset, tight timeframe. Starts from $110 for a studio or one bedroom.

Move-in / move-out

Empty unit, end of tenancy

An empty property cleaned to an inspection standard after someone has lived in it for months or years. Deeper, longer, and judged by a landlord or agent rather than a guest. Starts from $195.

The practical test is condition and emptiness. A furnished unit that was clean four days ago is a turnover. A unit handed back at the end of a tenancy — empty, with months of ordinary living behind it — is a move-out clean, and it costs more because it is more work.

There is a middle case worth naming: a short-term rental that a guest has genuinely trashed, or one that has sat unlet for a long stretch. That is not a turnover. Tell us what the state is and it will be quoted as the job it actually is, which is fairer than discovering the mismatch on arrival. Rental turnover cleaning covers how we handle both.

Tight Turnover Windows

Same-day checkout and check-in, and what is realistic.

The standard Airbnb pattern — checkout at 11, check-in at 15 — leaves about four hours, and that is comfortable for a maintained unit if the linen situation is sorted. It gets difficult when a checkout runs late, when laundry is on the critical path, or when two units turn over on the same morning.

A few things make tight windows work:

  • Spare linen sets, so no turnover waits on a dryer
  • Standing arrangements rather than ad-hoc bookings — a known schedule beats a scramble
  • Reliable access — a lockbox or code, so nothing waits on a handover
  • Telling us the window up front, so the visit is planned around the check-in time rather than squeezed
  • Flagging anything unusual — a party, a pet, a long stay — before the visit rather than after

Short-notice requests are normal in this work and we take them on when the schedule allows. Two honest caveats. We cannot guarantee a same-day slot — availability depends on what is already booked, and you will get a straight answer rather than a maybe. And short-notice work may carry rush pricing depending on how soon it is needed, which is quoted up front rather than appearing afterwards. If you need a unit turned around at very short notice, same-day and short-notice cleaning explains how that is handled.

Hosts running several units are better served by a standing arrangement than by booking each turnover separately. Send the addresses and the usual pattern and it can be quoted together.

What to Tell Your Cleaner

Five things that make the difference between a good turnover and a guessed one.

Most turnover problems are communication problems rather than cleaning problems. These are worth setting out once, in writing, so nobody has to improvise:

  • Where everything lives. Linen, spare towels, cleaning supplies, bin location, recycling day. A cleaner who has to hunt for the vacuum is burning your turnover window.
  • How access works. Lockbox code, key location, parking, which door. Changes to this are the most common cause of a missed slot.
  • What the presentation standard is. How the bed should be made, how towels should be folded, whether amenities get restocked and from where. Consistency is what guests notice.
  • The check-in time. Not just “today” — the actual time, so the visit can be planned backwards from it.
  • Anything out of the ordinary. A pet stay, a large group, a long booking, a guest who mentioned a spill. Thirty seconds of warning changes how the visit is scoped.

If you are a Kamloops host managing more than one property, it is worth writing this down once per unit rather than repeating it each time. It makes turnovers consistent, and consistency is what keeps reviews steady.
